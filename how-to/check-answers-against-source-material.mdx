A **Groundedness judge** answers one question: *is everything this reply asserts backed by the source material I gave it?* It splits each agent reply into individually checkable claims, checks each one against the documents attached to the scenario, and returns a per-claim report where every supported claim carries a quote the product verified against the document before showing it.

Use it when your agent answers from a policy, a rate sheet, a help-centre article, or any body of knowledge you can paste in — and a confidently-worded invention would be worse than no answer.

<Note>
  *Ask the Trust Agent:* "Create a Groundedness judge for the refund policy, attach it to the refund-timing scenarios, and tell me which of them still need reference material." It drafts the evaluator and the bindings, and shows you the write for [approval](/how-to/approve-agent-writes) before anything is saved. The screens below are the observation pane and the manual path.
</Note>

## What it refuses to judge

This is the most important thing to understand before you use it, because it is what makes the result trustworthy.

A Groundedness judge **only** decides whether a claim is backed by the material. It does not:

- **Penalise omissions.** Leaving a detail out is a coverage question, not a groundedness one.
- **Judge tone, style, or formatting.** A curt, badly-formatted, perfectly-grounded answer passes.

So a passing Groundedness check is a narrow, strong statement: *nothing here was made up*. It is not a statement that the answer was good — helpfulness and completeness are not inputs to its decision either, they simply are not among the boundaries the judge states. Pair it with an [LLM judge](/how-to/write-llm-judge-prompt) for everything it deliberately ignores — that combination is the point, not a workaround.

The judge states those two boundaries itself. Open one from the Evaluators list and its **Criteria** tab reads top to bottom: **How this judge works** (what it checks, then the two never-does lines above), **Verdict vocabulary**, the editable **Additional guidance**, and **Grades these scenarios** — the last of which lists every scenario this judge is bound to and flags the ones still reading *"No reference material"*, so you can find the unattached ones without opening each scenario.

## 1. Create the judge

On the Evaluators library, choose **New evaluator**. The **Kind** chooser offers exactly two cards — **LLM judge** and **Groundedness judge** — and picking the second one changes what the rest of the form asks for.

| An LLM judge asks for | A Groundedness judge asks for |
|---|---|
| Name, Description | Name, Description |
| **Score type** — Pass / fail or 1–5 scale | *(nothing — it is always pass/fail)* |
| **Judge criteria** — the rules you author | *(nothing — the rubric is built in and can't be edited)* |
| **Reason step by step** toggle | *(not offered)* |
| | **Additional guidance** — optional |

That is the whole difference: **you write no criteria and pick no score type.** The Score type cards and the Judge criteria textarea are not hidden-but-defaulted, they are gone from the form.

**Additional guidance** is optional free text appended to the built-in rubric — the place to note a domain quirk it should respect, not a place to restate the rubric. Leave it blank to grade with the standard rubric; you can add or clear it later from the evaluator's **Criteria** tab.

<Note>
  You get exactly **two** levers on this judge: the optional guidance above, and the reference material you attach to each scenario. Everything else — the rubric, the claim decomposition, the evidence rule — belongs to the product. See [Evaluators](/concepts/evaluators) for how it sits alongside the other kinds.
</Note>

## 2. Bind it to a scenario

Open a [Scenario](/concepts/scenarios) and go to its **Expected Output** tab. Under **Expected behavior**, use **Add evaluator** and pick your judge. The section's own subtitle states the gating rule — every bound evaluator has to pass for the scenario to pass — with one carve-out this judge can trigger: a check that lands on **Needs review** is excluded from pass/fail gating rather than counted as a failure. See [step 5](#5-read-the-result).

## 3. Attach the reference material

**Reference material lives on the scenario, never on the evaluator.** The same judge grades different scenarios against different sources, so the documents belong to the thing being graded.

Binding the judge is what makes the editor appear: a **Reference material** subsection nested under **Expected behavior**, which is not rendered at all until a scenario binds a judge that grades against documents. Until you attach one it shows a blocker:

> **Reference material required**
> *&lt;your judge&gt;* grades this scenario's answers against reference material. Attach at least one document — until then this scenario can't run.

**Add reference material** opens a three-field form:

| Field | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Yes | Also searchable evidence — the judge may quote the title itself |
| **Source link (optional)** | No | Renders as a **Source** link on the row; not read by the judge |
| **Content** | Yes | Paste the article, policy, or knowledge itself |

The limits are **5 documents per scenario** and **64 KB of text per document**, and both are enforced in the form rather than as a failed save:

- The content field shows a live size ("6 KB"). Past 64 KB it turns red, prints *"This document is too long — trim it to 64 KB or less"*, and **Add document** stays disabled.
- At five documents, **Add reference material** is disabled and the section prints *"Up to 5 documents per scenario."*

Attached documents list by title with their size and, when you gave one, a **Source** link. Each row can be edited or removed; the writes ride the scenario's Expected Output save.

## 4. Run — the launcher blocks before it starts

If a scenario binds a Groundedness judge and has no reference material, the run **does not start and is not queued**. The launcher disables its primary button — **Run evaluation** on a bulk launch, **Run & evaluate** when you launch a single scenario, **Queue run** when the environment you picked is busy — and names the problem inline:

> **Reference material required**
> *&lt;scenario&gt;* needs reference material before it can run — a Groundedness judge grades its answers against attached documents, but none are attached. Add reference material on its Expected Output tab.

More than one offending scenario is listed together in one message. The same rule is enforced server-side, so a run launched from the API is rejected the same way rather than starting and quietly skipping the check. The gate is specific to graded scenario runs: a run-only launch grades nothing, and a [session-source evaluation](/how-to/evaluate-sessions) doesn't inherit scenario judges.

Fix it on the scenario's **Expected Output** tab, then run again.

## 5. Read the result

The check renders as a card in the result fly-in, carrying a verdict, a metric chip, and a per-verdict tally of the claims.

**The verdict** is **Pass**, **Fail**, or **Needs review** — or, on a row where the judge had nothing to grade, no verdict at all. Needs review is a real third state, not a soft fail: it is the answer when the grade can't be trusted as-is, and it is excluded from pass/fail gating, so it neither passes the row silently nor paints it red.

A fourth, quieter state exists and is worth recognising. A Groundedness check is pinned to the whole evaluation, but reference material lives on each scenario — so on a run covering several scenarios, a row whose scenario doesn't bind the judge shows the check card ungraded: no verdict chip, a neutral (never red) accent, and the line *"No reference material attached — not graded."* That is honest bookkeeping, not a failure, and step 4's launcher gate does not prevent it — the gate only fires for a scenario that binds the judge *and* has no documents.

**The metric chip** reads **N% grounded** — the share of scored claims the judge could ground in the material. It sits next to the verdict as a neutral annotation, never in place of it.

**The counts line** tallies the claims by verdict, e.g. `14 supported · 1 unsupported · 1 unknown · 1 conversational`. Only non-zero tallies appear.

### How the verdict is decided

Grading is **strict**: the grounded fraction has to be 1.0. There is no tolerance dial.

| The claim set contains | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Any **Contradicted** claim | **Fail** — a contradiction is a hard violation, even alongside unknowns |
| Otherwise, any **Unknown** claim | **Needs review** — uncertainty is never quietly grounded |
| Otherwise, no scored claims at all | **Pass** — pure pleasantries, nothing to be ungrounded about |
| Otherwise, anything less than 100% supported | **Fail** — any single unsupported claim fails the answer |
| Otherwise | **Pass** |

**Conversational** claims — greetings and filler — are excluded from the denominator entirely.

## 6. Open the groundedness report

**View groundedness report** on the check card opens the evidence surface: claims on the left grouped by reply, the source on the right.

Every claim card carries the judge's **verdict**, its **reasoning in plain language**, and — for a supported or contradicted claim — the **verbatim quote** it was grounded in. Clicking a claim highlights that quote inside the document, and the right pane toggles between **Reference material** and **Agent's replies** so you can read a claim against either the source it was checked against or the sentence it came from.

The five claim verdicts are one vocabulary, used identically in the report and on the evaluator's **Criteria** tab:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Supported** | Directly backed by a verified quote from the reference material |
| **Contradicted** | The material says the opposite — always fails the check |
| **Unsupported** | A factual claim the material doesn't back |
| **Unknown** | The judge couldn't decide, or its evidence couldn't be verified — triggers review |
| **Conversational** | Greetings and filler — excluded from scoring |

### The quotes are checked, not asserted

This is the part worth trusting. A supported or contradicted claim must cite a quote, and the product searches for that quote — whitespace-normalised — in the cited document's title and content before the claim is allowed to keep its verdict. A quote that isn't found verbatim doesn't quietly stand: the claim is **downgraded to Unknown**, the card says what it was downgraded from and why, and the whole check lands on **Needs review**.

A fabricated citation therefore cannot prop up a pass. That is why the evidence in the report is checkable rather than merely claimed.

## 7. Override a claim, and revert

A reviewer can correct an individual claim. Click a claim's verdict chip to open **Override verdict**: it shows the judge's verdict, a **Your verdict** picker, and an optional **Why** note, then **Apply override**.

You may override a claim to **Supported**, **Contradicted**, **Unsupported**, or **Conversational** — **four** options. **Unknown** is deliberately not one of them: it is the judge's uncertainty sink, not a reviewer's call.

Applying an override **recomputes the score** through the same rule the judge used — the counts, the **N% grounded** figure, and the check's Pass / Fail / Needs review verdict all move, and the parent result's roll-up moves with them. Resolving the unknown claims is exactly how a Needs review check re-enters pass/fail gating. No model re-runs; the recompute is deterministic.

An overridden claim keeps a provenance strip reading *"Overridden by reviewer — judge said …"* with your note, plus **Revert**, which restores the judge's verdict and recomputes again.

<Note>
  This is the one place a human verdict rewrites an automated one. It does **not** generalise — see [Annotations](/concepts/annotations) for how session-level Verdict annotations differ.
</Note>

## When claims can't be adjudicated

Overrides need a claim list worth adjudicating, and only two check states have one: a check that finished grading, and a check that needs review *specifically* because of unknown claims. Every other route to Needs review is structural — a truncated or unparseable judge response, a claim set over the budget, a decomposition that failed a guard, or reference material edited while the run was in flight — and none of those leaves a claim list the product will let you adjudicate. On those the report offers no override affordance and asks you to re-run instead.
