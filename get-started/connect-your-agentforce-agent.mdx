This is the on-ramp for a team whose agent runs on **Salesforce Agentforce**. It takes you from nothing to a connected agent you can evaluate. When you're done:

- You can sign in to Trust AI and open a [Project](/concepts/projects)
- Your Salesforce org is prepared, and a Salesforce administrator has authorized TrustAI once
- One Agentforce agent is connected and ready to run

<Note>
  Two different "agents" meet on this page. The **Agentforce agent** you're connecting is *your* agent — the one under test, tracked as an [Agent Version](/concepts/agent-versions). The **Trust Agent** is the in-app assistant that helps you do the connecting. They share a word and nothing else.
</Note>

## Get access to Trust AI

Trust AI signs you in through your workspace's SSO identity provider. There's nothing for you to configure and no separate Trust AI password to manage.

- **Ask the Provar team to set up your workspace**, and tell them who the first workspace **Owner** should be.
- **After the workspace exists, access is self-service inside it.** The Owner or a workspace **Admin** invites the rest of your team and grants each person access to the right Project. See [Administer workspace access](/how-to/administer-workspace-access) for roles, invites, and per-project **Viewer** and **Editor** grants.

You also need a [Project](/concepts/projects) before you can connect anything — create one from the project switcher at the top of the sidebar. [Your first 10 minutes in Trust AI](/tutorials/quickstart) owns that walkthrough; its first step is exactly this.

## Prepare your Salesforce org

Work through this before you open the connection form. A missing item shows up later as a failed check.

**The agent**

- **The Agentforce agent must be activated**, not just created. Open it in **Agentforce Builder** and turn it on.

**The person who authorizes**

- **A Salesforce administrator has to complete the authorization step in person.** It's a one-time, in-browser approval.
- That administrator needs three Salesforce permissions: **Modify Metadata Through Metadata API Functions**, **Author Apex**, and **Customize Application**. TrustAI validates them before it writes anything to your org.
- **Have that administrator sign in as the user you want the agent to run as.** The connection runs as the administrator who authorized it.

**The user the agent runs as**

- **Assign the selected agent's own permission set** to that user in Salesforce. TrustAI assigns its own access permission set, but never the agent's.
- **The same user needs read access to agent metadata.** Without it, sign-in succeeds and the agent list comes back empty.

**The org**

- **Your org's URL** — the My Domain host, like `https://myorg.my.salesforce.com`. You'll paste it into **Salesforce instance URL**. A sandbox has its own My Domain, which looks like `https://myorg--dev.sandbox.my.salesforce.com`. If you paste a Lightning or Setup URL, Trust AI normalizes it to the right host.
- **Use a Sandbox, not production.** Authorizing deploys an **External Client App** into whichever org you sign in to, so point this at a Salesforce Sandbox. This is guidance, not a block.
- **Agentforce has to be enabled in the org.** Trust AI doesn't check this — there is no enablement probe anywhere in the connection path — so confirm it with your Salesforce admin first. {/* ACCURACY-AUDIT-PENDING: the failure SHAPE ("fails with a generic error rather than naming the cause") is runbook-attested only; verifying it needs an org without Agentforce, which no local fixture can produce. The no-probe half is code-verified. */}

<Note>
  *Ask the Trust Agent:* "What do I need to set up in Salesforce before I connect my Agentforce agent?" It walks the same checklist in plain language, so you can forward it to whoever holds the administrator account.
</Note>

### What TrustAI deploys

You hand over no consumer key, consumer secret, certificate, or password. When your administrator approves, TrustAI deploys that External Client App into the org, along with a permission set that authorizes the run-as user for it. TrustAI creates, names, and manages that app itself — you never see it in the connect flow.

That deployment happens on the **first** authorization of an org. A second Project pointed at the same org reuses the authorization the workspace already holds, so no admin signs in and no second app is deployed — see Step 1 below.

Removing the connection later is two different actions with different blast radii — read [Manage Salesforce connections](/how-to/manage-salesforce-connections) before you use either.

## Connect the agent

The fastest route is to ask the Trust Agent; the steps below are the manual path, in short. Full detail: [Connect a Salesforce Agentforce agent](/how-to/connect-agentforce).

<Note>
  *Ask the Trust Agent:* "Connect my Salesforce Agentforce agent." It checks whether something is already connected, asks for your org URL, and hands you the authorization card — or, if your workspace already authorized that org, offers to reuse it instead of signing anyone in. You don't name a run-as user on this path: a new authorization runs as the administrator who approves it.
</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a connection">
    In **Project Settings**, open the **Connections** tab and click **Connect an agent**, then choose **Salesforce Agentforce**. The wizard runs inside that tab: one header reading **Connect an agent**, and **Back to connections** as the way out of any step.

    Give the connection a **Connection Name** you'll recognize later — "Agentforce Sandbox" beats "Salesforce 1" — and enter your org's URL in **Salesforce instance URL**. There is no sign-in method to choose: authorizing as an admin is how a **new** org connection is created.

    **You should see:** the host Trust AI will use echoed back under the field.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Reuse an authorization, if there is one">
    If your workspace has already authorized this org — in this Project or another one you can reach — this step offers it before it asks anyone to sign in: **Reuse a Salesforce connection you already have**, with **Use this connection** beside each org.

    Take it. Reusing clones the connection into this Project pointed at the same org, with no agent chosen yet, so you skip straight to picking your agent. No administrator signs in, and no second External Client App is deployed. If there is nothing to reuse, this panel doesn't appear at all — carry on with the next step.

    **You should see:** either the reuse panel with your org listed, or the authorize button on its own.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Authorize once, in Salesforce">
    Click **Authorize in Salesforce with an administrator account**. Salesforce opens in a new tab, your administrator signs in and approves, and that tab closes itself.

    The Salesforce tab shows **Connecting** while Trust AI sets the org up. If the account that approved is missing one of the three administrator permissions, it stops on **An admin needs to finish setup**, usually naming the missing permissions. If it names none, hand the authorization to a System Administrator and try again.

    **You should see:** the original form return on its own and advance to the agent-picking step, headed **Salesforce org connected**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Pick your agent">
    Trust AI lists the Agentforce agents in the org by name — pick yours in **Agent**. If nothing is listed, click **Find agents** to run the search again. Leave **Run agent as** on its default — *Authenticated integration user (Employee Agent) (default)* — unless your agent is a Service Agent.

    **You should see:** your agent listed by name rather than by an identifier you had to look up.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Test, then save">
    Click **Test Connection** first. Trust AI signs in, lists your agents, and opens and immediately closes one real session against the agent you picked — it sends no message, so the test costs no agent turn. The form reports a single summary line; for the three checks behind it, and the exact message and fix for each failure, see [What the connection test checks](/how-to/connect-agentforce#what-the-connection-test-checks).

    Then click **Save changes**. Saving records your agent choice, and **Back to connections** returns you to the **Connections** tab — which is why you test first, since **Test Connection** lives on the wizard's step and a default build offers no route back into a saved connection's form.

    **You should see:** **Connection verified** from the test, then your connection on the **Connections** tab as a card — its name, a **Salesforce Agentforce** chip, the org host, and a green **Connected** reachability pill. Workspace Settings → **Connected orgs** shows the org with sign-in **Admin authorization**; a finished setup shows no setup badge there, just that same reachability pill.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to expect after connecting

- **The Trust Agent keeps going.** Connecting is the first step of a guided setup, not the end of one. It starts a background read of your agent's make-up — its topics, its actions, and the operating rules Salesforce declares for it — so you don't have to go looking for a capture action. See [Review an agent profile](/how-to/review-an-agent-profile). Two honest limits at this release: the agent's source instruction text does not cross the platform's API boundary, and exact action input schemas are unavailable for newer-generation Agentforce agents, which is why tool-schema coverage there reads **Partial**.
- **It drafts your first [Requirements](/concepts/requirements) from what your agent actually does.** Every draft is shown in the conversation, and the whole batch files under a single approval card — all-or-nothing, numbered as you read them. Its next-step suggestion follows your project's real state each turn rather than a fixed script.
- **A stock agent works without extra setup.** New connections verify identity conversationally rather than injecting a session variable an unmodified Agentforce agent rejects, and test data signs in as the agent connection itself, so nothing asks you for a second set of credentials.
- **Sessions come from Trust AI driving your agent** — observe-only explorations, simulated [Scenarios](/concepts/scenarios), and evaluation runs. Each lands in the Project as a [Session](/concepts/sessions). Historical import of past production conversations isn't available for Agentforce today: **Sync now** lists your Agentforce agent, then reports that sync isn't supported for the platform.
- **Step-level trace evidence needs extra setup in Salesforce.** To see the agent's internal steps rather than just the transcript, more has to be turned on org-side than the connection itself needs. Where it works, Data 360 is live and **Agentforce Session Tracing** and **Audit and Feedback** are on — Trust AI doesn't check for any of these and no message names them, so confirm with your Salesforce admin. {/* ACCURACY-AUDIT-PENDING: Data 360 provisioned and live, Agentforce Session Tracing, and Audit and Feedback are required for step-level trace evidence — attested only by an engineering runbook, with no code check or user-facing message. Verify against the app on the release tag */}
- **Traces settle late.** Salesforce publishes trace data on its own schedule — expect roughly 11 to 13 minutes, typically, before a run's step evidence is complete. Salesforce does not backfill sessions from before tracing was turned on.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Some checks pass and others don't">
    **Reach the selected agent** is the check that decides whether the connection works — a clean sign-in on its own isn't enough. The cause is almost always an item from [Prepare your Salesforce org](#prepare-your-salesforce-org): an agent that isn't activated, a run-as user missing the agent's own permission set, or a user without read access to agent metadata. The [failure list on the how-to](/how-to/connect-agentforce#troubleshooting) gives the exact message and fix for each.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Trust AI says this instance isn't configured to make Agentforce connections">
    Clicking **Authorize in Salesforce with an administrator account** answers with exactly that message instead of opening Salesforce. It means what it says: the admin-authorize flow is provisioned per deployment, and this instance's connected app hasn't been set up yet. Contact the Provar team rather than reaching for another sign-in method — there is no client-side workaround.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
