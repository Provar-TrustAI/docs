This how-to connects a **Salesforce Agentforce** agent to a Trust AI [Project](/concepts/projects). You sign in to Salesforce once as an administrator and approve TrustAI — there is no consumer key, no secret, and no certificate to hand over. When you're done:

- Trust AI can drive your live Agentforce agent: simulations and evaluation runs open a real session in your org, and those conversations land in the Project as [Sessions](/concepts/sessions)
- The agent you picked becomes the Project's current [Agent Version](/concepts/agent-versions), selectable when you launch an Evaluation

Agentforce is one of **four** platforms in the **Add a connection** picker, in this order: **AWS Bedrock AgentCore**, **Salesforce Agentforce**, **Claude Managed Agent**, **Conversational HTTP agent**. See [Connect an AWS Bedrock AgentCore agent](/how-to/connect-agentcore), [Connect a Claude Managed Agent](/how-to/connect-claude-managed-agent), and [Connect a Conversational HTTP agent](/how-to/connect-http-agent) for the other three. This page covers Agentforce only.

<Warning>
  **Use a Sandbox, not production.** Approving TrustAI sets up a connected app in the org you sign in to, so only connect this to a Salesforce Sandbox. The **Project Settings** connect form repeats this above the authorize button; the in-chat card does not.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Setting Trust AI up for the first time? [From zero to a connected Salesforce agent](/get-started/connect-your-agentforce-agent) is the shorter on-ramp — how you get access, what to prepare in your Salesforce org before you start, and this connection in brief. Come back here for the full detail.
</Note>

## Prerequisites

- A **Salesforce Sandbox** with an Agentforce agent that is **activated**. An agent that exists but has never been turned on lists fine and then refuses to open a session — see [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting).
- A **Salesforce administrator** — you, or someone who can sign in while you wait. This is a one-time sign-in, not an ongoing role.
- Your org's URL, for example `https://myorg--dev.sandbox.my.salesforce.com`. Whatever is in your browser address bar works; TrustAI trims it to the org and shows you the host it will connect to.
- A **Trust AI Project**. If you don't have one, start with [Your first 10 minutes in Trust AI](/tutorials/quickstart).

You do **not** need a consumer key, a consumer secret, a certificate, or a private key. When the admin approves, TrustAI deploys its own **External Client App** into the org (named `TrustAI_Agent_Eval_…`) and keeps the credentials server-side.

## Connect in chat with the Trust Agent

The fastest route is to ask the [Trust Agent](/concepts/trust-agent) — try *"Connect my Salesforce Agentforce agent."* It completes the whole connection in the conversation: it first checks whether an agent is already connected, then opens a secure connection card. Nothing you type in the card is visible to the model or stored in the chat.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your org URL">
    The card opens on **Your Salesforce org** (**Step 1 of 3**, with **Org · Sign-in · Review** as the progress chips). Paste your org URL into **Salesforce instance URL**. The card echoes the host it will use — *"We'll connect to `myorg--dev.sandbox.my.salesforce.com`"* — then click **Continue**.

    If that org is already connected in this Project, the card says so and offers **Use this connection** instead of a second authorization. If it's connected in another of your Projects, it offers to reuse that connection here.

    **You should see:** the org preview line resolve to your Sandbox host, and **Continue** become clickable.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Authorize TrustAI">
    On **How do you want to sign in?**, the lead option is **Authorize TrustAI (recommended)** — *"Sign in once as a Salesforce admin and approve TrustAI — no keys, no secret, no certificate to share."* It is selected by default. Click **Authorize in Salesforce**.

    Salesforce opens in a new tab. Approve TrustAI with an administrator account, and that tab brings you back to this conversation — the Trust Agent picks up where it left off and confirms the connection is live.

    **You should see:** the Salesforce login open in a new tab, and your chat resume by itself once the approval lands.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose your agent">
    A freshly provisioned connection has no agent selected yet, so the Trust Agent follows up with the in-chat agent picker. TrustAI lists the agents in that org — by name, never a raw id — and you pick the one to test. Your choice is saved onto the connection that was just created; nothing is reconnected.

    **You should see:** your org's agents listed by name, and the one you pick recorded against the connection.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read the connection check">
    The card finishes on the connection readout: **Connection verified** (green) with *"Signed in, found your agents, and reached the one you picked"*, or **Connected, but a few things need your attention** (amber) with a **To finish setting up** list. Fix anything listed org-side and click **Re-check permissions**, or click **Done**.

    **You should see:** three named checks with a one-line result each — see [What the connection test checks](#what-the-connection-test-checks).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connect from Project Settings

The same connection, without the chat. On the create path this is a **two-step wizard**: authorize first, pick your agent second. There is no sign-in-method choice to make here — the admin-authorize path *is* the create path.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the connection form">
    In your Project, open **Project Settings**, select the **Connection** tab, and click **Add connection**. On **Add a connection**, choose **Salesforce Agentforce**.

    The platform is fixed from here: the form's header is a breadcrumb (**Connections › Add › Salesforce Agentforce**) with a **Change** link back to the picker, not a platform selector you set on the form.

    **You should see:** **New connection** with a **Salesforce Agentforce** chip, and exactly two fields — **Connection Name** and **Salesforce instance URL**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Name it, then authorize">
    Give the connection a **Connection Name** you'll recognize later, like `Agentforce Sandbox`, and enter your org URL in **Salesforce instance URL**. TrustAI echoes the host it will connect to underneath.

    Click **Authorize in Salesforce with an administrator account**. Salesforce opens in a new tab and the form tells you so: *"We've opened Salesforce in a new tab. Approve TrustAI with an administrator account — this page continues automatically once the connection is ready."*

    There is no **Save** on this step, and nothing to test yet — the connection is created in your org when the admin approves. The Salesforce tab closes itself when it's finished, and the form you started in advances to step 2 on its own.

    **You should see:** the sandbox caution above the button, and **Cancel** as the only other control.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Pick the agent and save">
    Step 2 opens with **Salesforce org connected — `myorg--dev.sandbox.my.salesforce.com`. Now choose the agent to test.** TrustAI runs **Find agents** for you against the saved connection, tells you how many it found, and lists them by name in **Agent** — pick yours. If discovery can't reach your org, **Enter agent ID manually** is still there as an escape hatch.

    Leave **Run agent as** on its default — *Authenticated integration user (Employee Agent)* — unless your agent is a Service Agent, which runs as its own assigned user.

    Click **Save changes**. (The button reads **Save changes**, not "Save connection": the connection already exists, and saving records your agent choice onto it rather than creating a second one.)

    **You should see:** the connection listed on the **Connection** tab with its platform, and your chosen agent as the **Current agent version**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Test the connection">
    **Test Connection** sits beside **Save changes** on step 2, and on the connection's **Edit** form afterwards. TrustAI signs in to your org, lists its agents, and opens one real session against the agent you picked, then closes it. Nothing is sent to the agent, so the test costs no conversation and leaves none behind.

    **You should see:** **Connection verified**, or a specific line naming what stopped it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the connection test checks

A Salesforce connection is not one pass/fail. A partial permission grant can let TrustAI sign in but not list agents, or list agents but not reach the one you picked — so the test runs three ordered checks and reports each on its own.

| # | Check | What it proves |
| - | ----- | -------------- |
| 1 | **Sign in to Salesforce** | The connection's credentials resolve against your org. If this fails, the two checks below are skipped rather than guessed at. |
| 2 | **List Agentforce agents** | The connected user can read agent metadata. Signed in but nothing listed is a permission gap, not a broken connection. |
| 3 | **Reach the selected agent** | The chosen agent actually opens a session. Listing an agent is not evidence it can run — an inactive agent, or a run-as user without the agent's permission set, enumerates cleanly and then refuses. With no agent chosen yet, this check is reported as skipped, not failed. |

Where the three checks render as three rows depends on where you ran the test:

- **In the Trust Agent card**, the full readout renders: a green **Connection verified** or an amber **Connected, but a few things need your attention**, each check with its own one-line result, plus a **To finish setting up** list of the specific fixes.
- **On the Settings form**, **Test Connection** reports the single summary line instead — *"Connection verified"*, *"TrustAI signed in, but some checks need attention. See the details below."*, or the sentence naming the check that stopped it.

A connection whose selected agent cannot open a session is not verified, however clean the first two checks were — every run launched against it would die with no session.

## Where Agentforce sessions come from

Agentforce sessions in Trust AI are the conversations **Trust AI drives**: simulations and evaluation runs against the live agent in your Sandbox.

Historical import is a different thing and it is not available for Agentforce. Your connection does appear in the **Sync now** dropdown on the Sessions page, grouped under **Salesforce Agentforce** — but picking it reports that sync isn't supported for the platform and imports nothing. Pulling past production conversations works for AWS Bedrock connections only today.

## Legacy sign-in methods

In **Project Settings**, the **Sign-in method** selector appears only when you **edit** an existing connection, where it offers, in order:

- **Authorize TrustAI as an admin (recommended)**
- **External Client App key + secret (legacy)**
- **Certificate-based JWT (legacy)**
- **Username + password (legacy)**

Use it to keep an older connection working, or to move one onto the recommended path.

The chat card is the one exception, and it is worth knowing so you can walk past it: on a **new** connection, its sign-in step carries a collapsed **Advanced / legacy sign-in options** link that reveals two of the legacy modes, labelled **Key and secret** and **Certificate (JWT)** there. Leave it collapsed. **Authorize TrustAI (recommended)** is already selected, and each legacy mode asks you to hand over credentials the admin authorization never needs.

Secrets on the legacy modes stay write-only: a saved value shows as **Configured** with the placeholder `•••• configured — leave blank to keep existing`, and a blank field keeps what's already stored.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The agent isn't activated">
    The test reports: *"TrustAI signed in, but this Salesforce agent isn't activated, so no session could start."* The fix it names is **"Open the agent in Agentforce Builder and turn it on, then test the connection again."**

    This is the most common real cause, and it is invisible in the agent list — an inactive agent still enumerates, so the picker shows it as a normal choice. The message you see at run time is worded differently on purpose, because by then the next step is to run again rather than to re-test.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Signed in, but no agents are listed">
    *"TrustAI signed in, but could not list any Agentforce agents. The connected user is likely missing read access to agent metadata."* Grant the connected Salesforce user read access to agent metadata — for example a permission set that exposes the org's Agentforce agents — then test the connection again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The run-as user can't open the selected agent">
    *"TrustAI signed in, but the run-as user can't open the selected agent. The user is missing the agent's permission set."* Assign the selected agent's permission set to that user in Salesforce, then test again. The connection itself is fine — this is an org-side grant.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Starting a session as the run-as user was refused">
    *"TrustAI signed in, but starting a session as the run-as user was refused. The connected app isn't issuing named-user tokens yet, so the agent can't run as your chosen user."* Ask your Salesforce admin to turn on named-user token issuance for the External Client App used for this connection, then test again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Salesforce opened, but the form never continued">
    The form advances when the authorization lands, so a form still sitting on step 1 means the approval didn't complete — a non-admin account, a cancelled consent screen, or a closed tab. Click **Authorize in Salesforce with an administrator account** again to restart it. If the kickoff itself fails, the reason appears inline above the button rather than as a silently dead click.

    In chat, the recovery is the same card: ask the Trust Agent to connect again and authorize from there.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Sync now reports that sync isn't supported">
    Expected — see [Where Agentforce sessions come from](#where-agentforce-sessions-come-from). Your Agentforce sessions are the ones Trust AI creates by driving the agent.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Connections, their platform config, and the connection test are also readable on the wire; request and response shapes live in the [API Reference](/api-reference).
